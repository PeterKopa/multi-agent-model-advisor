# Quality scoring

Reviewed requirements for [issue #1](https://github.com/PeterKopa/multi-agent-model-advisor/issues/1). Confirmed decisions are normative; open design checks are not verified capabilities.

## Benchmark normalization (confirmed design decision)

- Normalize each benchmark using a benchmark-specific, documented and versioned transformation to a common 0–100 scale. Respect score direction, units, methodology and valid bounds; directly convert established bounded scales and use justified fixed reference values for other metrics.
- Normalization rules must be independent of the currently selected model set. Adding or removing candidates must not change normalized scores when the evidence and rules are unchanged.
- Preserve original measurements and the normalization rule/version used. Benchmarks without a defensible transformation remain visible as raw evidence but do not contribute to the combined quality score; explain the limitation and distinguish it from missing model measurements.
- Define exact transformations, reference values, handling of out-of-range values and compatibility between benchmark versions in the scoring-design task. Do not fabricate normalized results.

## Benchmark coverage and local-model evidence (confirmed design decision)

- Calculate benchmark coverage as the sum of effective role-specific weights for benchmarks with usable, comparable measurements for the model/configuration, divided by the total weight of the active scorable benchmarks. Zero-weight benchmarks do not count. Explain any benchmark excluded because it lacks a defensible normalization rule. Coverage is not a probability of task success.
- Provide a configurable minimum weighted benchmark coverage, defaulting to 70%. Models meeting the threshold may receive a quality score and ranking, subject to comparability and the scoring methodology. Show coverage and missing measurements explicitly; flag partial-evidence comparisons. Below the threshold, retain the model in a separate visible unranked/insufficient-evidence state; do not invent a score or make a benchmark-based standard recommendation. No usable measurements or no positive active weights means no score.
- For hosted model variants in the MVP and locally executed models in a later release, distinguish matching benchmark evidence from reference measurements of a different quantization, version, reasoning configuration or execution setup. Show reference measurements for orientation with clear provenance and mismatch labels, but do not count them toward coverage or use them as measured scores for the target configuration without supported comparability. Do not infer local performance from cloud or full-precision results.
- Keep missing benchmark evidence independent of route eligibility metadata. Future user-initiated model tests (#2) can supplement practical evidence but do not automatically fill gaps in public benchmark scores.

## Quality score and optional benchmark requirements (confirmed design decision)

- Calculate the quality score as the weighted arithmetic mean of normalized, comparable benchmark measurements using the approved positive role-specific weights. For models meeting the configured weighted coverage threshold (70% by default), renormalize the available weights over usable measurements only; do not substitute zeros for missing measurements. Show effective weights, coverage and the limitations of comparisons based on different evidence subsets. No score when the denominator is zero or the minimum coverage is not met.
- Allow optional per-agent minimum normalized scores for individual benchmarks as mandatory requirements for standard-model recommendations. These requirements are separate from the aggregate score; a high combined score cannot compensate for a failed mandatory benchmark. A missing comparable measurement for a mandatory benchmark is unresolved, not a pass; do not recommend the affected model as the standard.
- Preserve quality scores and ranking positions regardless of optional benchmark requirement failures, with visible reasons and comparison-only status where applicable. Existing mandatory route filters and default-model preferences remain independent. Validate thresholds against the normalized scale and require a usable normalization rule; clarify conflicts such as zero-weight benchmarks with mandatory requirements in the scoring design.
- With no individual benchmark requirements, use the same weighted arithmetic mean and existing eligibility rules. Define deterministic tie handling, precision and comparisons across partially overlapping evidence in the scoring-design task.

## Model ranking and default-model recommendation preferences

- For every selected model offering, allow the user to specify whether it may be recommended as the standard/default model or should remain a comparison candidate only (e.g. an expensive model reserved for exceptional use). Exclusion from the candidate set remains a separate selection decision. Use clear localized labels such as "Eligible as default" / "Als Standardmodell vorschlagen" and "Comparison only (not as default)" / "Nur im Vergleich (nicht als Standardmodell)".
- Rank distinct model identities and comparable model configurations, rather than individual backend routes, in a single model-centric ranking (e.g. top five distinct models). Show the selected backends/access routes as secondary information under each model, with expandable route-specific pricing, availability, filter status and default eligibility where relevant. Group routes only when the model/version/configuration match is supported; keep materially different or unresolved variants distinct. The default-model preference must not change benchmark weights, quality scores, cost values, ranking order, or rank numbers.
- Independently identify the economically recommended model and qualifying route(s) using the per-agent quality requirement and reference-cost strategy confirmed in questions 6–8, subject to mandatory filters and default eligibility. Show which eligible backend(s) can serve the recommended model; if multiple routes qualify, retain their differences and do not invent a cheapest route when costs are incomparable. A restriction on one route does not automatically exclude another route for the same model. Do not reorder the model-centric quality ranking based on cost. For example, if Fable ranks first but is comparison only and Sol ranks second and qualifies as a default, retain their quality ranks; recommend Sol only if it wins the configured economic strategy. If no candidate qualifies, show that no standard recommendation is available; do not silently relax restrictions. The standard candidate must be selected from the full applicable ranking, even if outside the displayed top five.
- For the MVP, rank models using their default reasoning/thinking effort (the effective default of the configured access route), without recommending or configuring separate effort levels. Record benchmark reasoning settings as test metadata; do not equate explicitly elevated or unknown effort with the default without supporting evidence, or fabricate default-effort scores. Flag non-comparable evidence and route-specific default differences. Explicit reasoning-effort recommendations are deferred until supported by the relevant agent configuration and adapter.
- Support a project-wide default eligibility preference per concrete backend/model offering, with optional agent-specific refinements and notes. Preserve preferences on refresh. Do not assume that an expensive model is excluded as a default unless the user chooses that preference.
- Keep the advisor recommendation-only; automatic model switching, task routing and budget enforcement are outside the initial scope.

## Incomplete evidence and ordering

- Retain weighted arithmetic mean over usable measurements and configurable 70% minimum weighted coverage. Use the same active scorable benchmark set and weights for a role. Identify non-normalizable benchmarks separately; an invalid mandatory requirement must not silently disappear.
- Expose mathematical bounds for a complete score: for coverage c (fraction) and observed mean s, lower = c*s, upper = c*s + (1-c)*100. These are possible extrema, not confidence intervals, measured scores or imputation. At full coverage both equal s. Keep the primary quality rank unchanged.
- Offer a pairwise common-benchmark comparison with weights renormalized over the intersection of comparable measurements; show common coverage and do not compare if no positive usable weight. Do not replace primary ranking. When bounds overlap or evidence differs, warn that an apparent advantage may not be established; don't label a model definitively superior solely on partial evidence. Establish recommendation comparability and tie policy in the scoring-design task before implementation; no automatic preference for missing data.
- Quality ordering uses only the score (deterministic tie handling); policy filters govern candidate admission and recommendation eligibility, not scores/rank order among admitted candidates. Soft preferences may be displayed separately or explicitly used in recommendation policy, never reorder the quality ranking. Define priority of soft preferences vs least-cost strategy before implementation; default is least comparable reference cost satisfying requirements.
- Keep practical test and judge results separate from public benchmark coverage and scores. No automatic score contribution. Open: exact normalization transforms, benchmark compatibility, default-effort evidence availability, tie precision, and recommendation handling when evidence subsets are materially different. Validate using representative real records in Phase 0.
