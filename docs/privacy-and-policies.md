# Privacy and eligibility

Reviewed requirements for [issue #1](https://github.com/PeterKopa/multi-agent-model-advisor/issues/1). Confirmed decisions are normative; open design checks are not verified capabilities.

## Model eligibility and provider policies

- Treat model identity separately from a concrete offering (provider, endpoint/routing option, model version and applicable tariff). A provider may offer both eligible and ineligible models or endpoints.
- Support user-configured allowlists and denylists at provider, model and offering level, including explicit organization-approved models/providers. Allow global defaults, project-specific restrictions and optional agent-specific restrictions; more restrictive policies take precedence and mandatory policies cannot be silently relaxed.
- Treat data retention/privacy independently from cost: allow users to require verified Zero Data Retention (ZDR), prefer ZDR, allow either ZDR or non-ZDR, or explicitly filter for non-ZDR offerings. Do not infer price, privacy compliance or organizational approval from a model name or provider alone. Represent unknown or unverified retention status separately; unknown is not verified eligible when verified ZDR is required; user-confirmed conditional comparison is governed by the incomplete-metadata policy.
- Support independent cost filters based on verified free offerings, paid usage allowed, and optional reference-cost limits where comparable cost data is available. For the MVP, defer subscription-specific cost filters and allowance-based optimization (question 7). Distinguish genuinely zero-priced offerings, hosted reference prices and unknown cost (local execution is future scope); never label unknown costs as free or interpret a reference-cost limit as an actual billing guarantee.
- Apply mandatory eligibility filters before eligible recommendations, including any declared technical requirements; unresolved offerings may be included in the same model-centric quality ranking after explicit user confirmation, with clear comparison-only status and route-specific reasons; they cannot qualify for the standard recommendation unless at least one selected route satisfies the mandatory filters. Soft preferences are visible separately and may influence explicitly configured recommendation policy, never quality rank or score; mandatory restrictions always take precedence. See scoring-methodology.md for the ordering invariant. Show the eligible offerings, excluded offerings with reasons, and an explicit no-match state; never silently relax a restriction.
- Store policies locally and allow users to inspect/edit them. Apply relevant privacy/approval restrictions to the optional LLM analysis path as well; if CLI routing cannot verify a mandatory restriction, do not assume compliance. The advisor is a recommendation tool, not an enforcement or compliance certification system.
- Preserve provenance and timestamps for offering-specific availability, price and retention metadata; expose unknown/stale values. Do not assume that benchmark measurements for a model establish the characteristics of every serving endpoint. ZDR is an offering/endpoint/routing property, not an intrinsic model property: the same model may be available with verified ZDR through provider A and without ZDR through provider B, or with different retention options within one provider. Never propagate a ZDR status between routes when grouping models. With a mandatory ZDR filter, only routes with sufficient ZDR evidence qualify for standard recommendations; non-ZDR routes are excluded from eligible recommendations, and unknown routes may be included for comparison only under the existing confirmation rules. Display the status and evidence separately for each route, and retain the model's shared quality ranking when another selected eligible route exists.

## Local secret handling

Use an OS-protected credential store for explicitly approved persistent OpenRouter data credentials; otherwise keep the key session-only in process memory (default). Transparently display the effective store, logical item identifier and an actual path only where exposed and applicable, without exposing secret contents or inventing filesystem locations. The displayed identifier must match the entry actually used for storage, retrieval and deletion. Never store key values in YAML, ordinary app config, caches, reports, exports, logs, telemetry, error messages, test fixtures or repository files. Do not use plaintext fallback or custom cryptography with a hardcoded key. Do not pass the credential to other CLI processes or external LLMs. Redact authorization headers and sensitive errors. Clear access on disconnect, delete the application's saved credential when asked, and clarify that provider-side revocation is separate. Require explicit network action, official HTTPS origin and TLS certificate verification. Security design follows the principles of dedicated secret storage, least privilege and safe logging in OWASP's Secrets Management and Key Management guidance (https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Secrets_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html and https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Key_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html); implementation and platform protection require validation, not a compliance claim.

Minimize plaintext lifetime, copies and exposure of credentials in process memory, including input widgets and HTTP authorization headers. Clear mutable buffers where practical; Go garbage collection and library copies prevent a general guarantee of immediate erasure. Assess platform-supported memory locking, swap and crash-dump protections during design; report actual protection and failures accurately. Do not claim RAM encryption or zeroization unless demonstrated. Encryption with a decryption key resident in the same process does not protect against arbitrary process-memory access. Avoid custom cryptography. Reference: https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Secrets_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html.

### Session memory protection (confirmed design decision)

Use Go standard-library AES-256-GCM (crypto/aes, crypto/cipher, crypto/rand) to encrypt the API credential between requests as defense in depth, not as a guarantee of encrypted RAM. Generate a fresh cryptographically random session key and ensure nonce uniqueness for each encryption under that key. Do not introduce OpenSSL or libsodium dependencies for this feature. Memory locking and crash-dump protections remain separate platform-specific design checks. Avoid external subprocesses for handling secrets and custom cryptography. Assess where the encryption key and expanded key state reside, plaintext copies in TUI/HTTP libraries, swap and crash dumps, cleanup behavior, supported OSes and failure paths. A same-process key cannot defeat arbitrary process-memory access. Decrypt only when needed for approved HTTPS authentication; minimize plaintext lifetime and clear buffers where feasible. Use synthetic credentials only in validation. The Go standard-library approach is confirmed; implementation, security validation and failure handling remain open. If encryption initialization or authentication fails, reject use of the affected credential rather than silently retain or use an unencrypted session credential. Sources: https://pkg.go.dev/crypto/cipher, https://docs.openssl.org/3.6/man3/OPENSSL_secure_malloc/, https://doc.libsodium.org/doc/memory_management.

## Geographic eligibility and data residency

- Support independently configurable geographic allowlists and denylists for model developer origin, provider legal domicile, inference/data-processing locations, and storage/retention locations where applicable. Do not infer one from another. Separately represent the regions from which a service is accessible.
- Allow country-level restrictions (e.g. CN, US, DE) and explicitly defined geographic groups (e.g. EU, EEA, Europe); distinguish EU from geographic Europe. Support examples such as EU-only processing, US-only processing, excluding China-based developers or processing, and unrestricted geography. Combine these with existing organization, privacy, cost and technical policies.
- Evaluate geographic requirements for the concrete serving endpoint and routing configuration, including possible fallback regions and applicable subprocessors when relevant. For a mandatory location restriction, all possible applicable locations must satisfy the policy; unknown, unverified or stale information must not be interpreted as proof of compliance. Local execution is a separate option and its location must not be assumed.
- Record metadata provenance, verification status and timestamps. Explain which geographic rule excludes an offering. Apply relevant geographic requirements to optional LLM CLI analysis as well; do not assume an adapter can guarantee a location if routing is not verifiable.
- Allow geographic policies at global, project and agent levels, preserving mandatory organization restrictions. Geographic eligibility is independent of ZDR and price and does not itself certify legal compliance.

## User confirmation for incomplete eligibility metadata

- Evaluate each active filter as satisfied, not satisfied, unknown/unverified, or not applicable. When relevant metadata is missing, stale or unverifiable, place the offering in a pending-review state instead of silently excluding it from all analysis or assuming it satisfies the filter.
- Present the exact missing fields, affected filters, available evidence and consequences to the user. Offer explicit actions to supply/correct evidence, exclude the offering, or include it conditionally in the benchmark comparison despite the unresolved metadata. Support individual and explicitly reviewed bulk confirmations.
- Distinguish eligibility for comparison from verified eligibility for recommendation/deployment. A conditional inclusion permits local quality scoring and comparison, but does not establish ZDR, geographic compliance, availability, price, technical capability or organization approval. Label the offering and its results as conditionally included/unverified; do not present it as an unconditionally compliant recommendation or allow its use for external LLM analysis when mandatory policies cannot be verified. Known policy violations remain excluded from eligible recommendations; mandatory organization restrictions cannot be overridden by a shortlist confirmation.
- Persist the user's decision locally with the project, scoped to the specific offering/version and unresolved criteria, with timestamp and optional rationale. Preserve the original unknown metadata. Re-evaluate decisions when relevant metadata, offering configuration or policies change; require renewed confirmation where necessary. Allow the user to revoke the decision.
- Keep missing eligibility metadata separate from missing benchmark measurements. Conditional inclusion must not invent benchmark values, assume zero costs, or change the scoring coverage calculation.

## Developer-friendly filter terminology

- Present the feature primarily as model selection filters and personal/project requirements, not as a compliance management system. Users need not know or configure formal company policies; allow simple model/provider selection and optional advanced restrictions.
- Use plain-language localized statuses such as "Matches your filters" (Entspricht deinen Filtern), "Information missing" (Angaben fehlen), "Manually included for comparison" (Manuell zum Vergleich aufgenommen), and "Does not match your filters" (Entspricht nicht deinen Filtern). Explain the exact criterion and evidence behind each status.
- Let developers record models explicitly approved by their organization if they know them, without asking them to certify legal or organizational compliance. A user's decision to include an offering in a comparison is not an assertion that the missing facts are true. Distinguish user preferences from explicitly configured mandatory restrictions.
- Default to a simple selection workflow with optional advanced settings for retention, geography and organization restrictions. Do not show an offering as company-compliant merely because it matches configured filters; the tool only evaluates known data against those filters.

## Inherited upstream filters and discovery scope

- Treat CLI/backend inventory as a potentially scoped view of the user's existing configuration; restrictions may affect only request-time routing and not catalog visibility. Allow the user to record inherited restrictions per discovery source/backend (e.g. non-ZDR offerings already excluded). Import supported non-secret filter metadata where available, with provenance; otherwise record the restriction as user-declared or unknown.
- Distinguish inherited inventory scope from additional Advisor filters. Do not silently expand a configured inventory to the full public provider catalog or reintroduce entries known to be excluded upstream. Do not infer that upstream routing restrictions have already filtered the inventory. Public catalog enrichment may add metadata to discovered entries, but adding new candidates requires explicit user selection and must respect configured mandatory restrictions.
- Distinguish inventory membership from endpoint-level evidence: a model being listed in a ZDR-filtered configuration does not alone prove all routes satisfy ZDR. Preserve unknown/unverified states, record any user-declared restriction separately, and use the existing conditional comparison workflow when relevant metadata cannot be established. An inherited routing constraint may be considered when evaluating the specific configured access route if supported by evidence.
- Display the source and known scope of each discovered inventory, including active inherited and additional filters. Refresh within the same configuration context and flag changes or uncertainty without silently changing approved candidates.

## Catalog visibility versus request-time routing eligibility

- A CLI/backend inventory may be unfiltered, partially filtered or fully scoped by upstream restrictions; never assume listing membership proves that request-time routing is allowed. Some providers expose models in a catalog even when account, key or request-level policies prevent inference.
- Represent inventory scope and request-time routing restrictions independently. Record whether each restriction affects catalog visibility, inference routing, both or an unknown stage. User-declared ZDR routing requirements are not evidence that the discovered list has already been filtered.
- Distinguish public endpoint ZDR capability from the effective ZDR enforcement of the user's configured route (account, workspace, administrator guardrails, key, client or request settings). For OpenRouter, public endpoint/catalog data does not establish the user's effective private settings; ordinary CLI discovery is not sufficient to determine them. Do not assume other providers expose these settings. Allow user/admin-declared enforcement per backend/configuration context, optionally scoped to model groups or specific offerings, with provenance and an explicit unknown state. Keep declared settings distinct from independently verified configuration evidence; a declaration is not proof that every endpoint is ZDR-capable or that a specific request will succeed. Do not require administrative credentials or privileged APIs for discovery. Where a mandatory ZDR requirement cannot be established to the required evidence standard, retain comparison-only status under the existing rules. Do not label an unknown setting as disabled or claim independently verified ZDR from a declaration alone.
- Where available, enrich discovered entries with official read-only eligibility metadata (e.g. OpenRouter's ZDR-filtered model catalog and ZDR endpoint registry). Intersect relevant endpoint evidence with known backend/account/request constraints; the existence of a ZDR endpoint alone does not guarantee the configured route can access it.
- Show discovered but known-ineligible offerings separately from eligible and unresolved offerings. Use the existing user-confirmed conditional comparison workflow for unknown data, not for overriding known violations. Never disable routing restrictions or automatically send inference requests merely to test eligibility; any future live validation would require a separate explicit action.
- Refresh eligibility metadata independently of inventory, and re-evaluate affected user selections when endpoint policies change. Preserve unknown account-specific availability, quota and routing states.

## MVP policy boundary

Use simple provider/model selection by default, advanced ZDR/geography filters when needed. Evaluate declared metadata and known applicable routing constraints, not an exhaustive automated audit of subprocessors or private account settings. Unknown mandatory evidence means comparison-only or exclusion, never verified compliance. Apply the same boundary to assistance, test execution and judging; no tool-enabled or uncontrolled execution of external prompts. Local-model policy evaluation is future scope.
